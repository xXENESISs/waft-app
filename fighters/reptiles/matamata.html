Matamata

Chelus fimbriata
RegionAmazon and Orinoco basins
HabitatSlow-moving freshwater, flooded forest and swamp environments
DisciplineBrazilian Jiu-Jitsu

The Matamata (Chelus fimbriata) is a highly specialised freshwater turtle from South America, recognised by its broad triangular head, rough shell, fringed skin and exceptional camouflage. It hunts by remaining almost motionless among submerged debris, then suddenly extending its long neck and using powerful suction to draw prey into its mouth. Its tubular snout also allows it to breathe at the surface while keeping most of its body concealed.

Martial discipline

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu is a grappling martial art that developed in Brazil during the 20th century from judo and earlier Japanese jujutsu traditions. It focuses on taking an opponent to the ground, establishing positional control and finishing with submission holds such as joint locks and chokes. Technique, timing, leverage and body positioning are central to the system, allowing practitioners to control opponents without relying primarily on striking or raw strength.

Core mechanics
Passive ability

Immobile Stalk

Each time the opponent uses Concentration, Matamata gains 1 stalk charge. At 4 charges, starting on the next turn, its next attack cannot miss, deals double damage, and absorbs 20 stamina from the opponent.

Special attack · Defensive · 4 hits

Ancestral Retreat

Withdraws into its shell, restores 60 HP and 30 stamina. During that turn, the next direct damage received is reduced by 50%, and 25% of the original incoming damage is reflected back to the attacker.
